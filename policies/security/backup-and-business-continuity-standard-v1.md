# Backup and Business Continuity Standard

**Organization:** Igzibo Engineering Solutions, Inc.  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Living policy  
**Effective Date:** September 16, 2026  
**Owner:** Igzibo Engineering Solutions, Inc.  
**Review Cycle:** At least annually and after material changes to applicable law, client requirements, technology, or business operations.

## 1. Purpose

Establishes baseline requirements for protecting critical information and maintaining or restoring important business and technology operations after disruption.

## 2. Scope

Business systems, technology systems, critical project information, client information where contractually applicable, source code, business records, and personnel responsible for continuity.

## Definitions

**Backup:** A separate copy of information or system state maintained to support recovery.
**BCP (Business Continuity Plan):** A documented plan for maintaining or restoring important business functions during and after a disruption.
**BIA (Business Impact Analysis):** An analysis used to identify important functions, dependencies, impacts, and recovery priorities.
**DR (Disaster Recovery):** The coordinated process for restoring technology, systems, data, or services after disruption.
**Igzibo:** Igzibo Engineering Solutions, Inc.
**RPO (Recovery Point Objective):** The maximum acceptable data loss measured in time.
**RTO (Recovery Time Objective):** The target time within which a system, service, or function should be restored.

## 3. Core Principles

Identify critical dependencies; maintain appropriate backups; protect backups from unauthorized access and alteration; test recovery; document responsibilities; and prioritize client and contractual requirements.

## 4. Business Impact and Recovery Objectives

Assess business, client, legal, financial, security, personnel, technology, and vendor impacts. Establish RTO and RPO targets appropriate to risk.

## 5. Backup and Security

Critical information should be backed up with appropriate frequency, retention, integrity, availability, confidentiality, and independence from the primary system. Protect backups against unauthorized deletion, alteration, and ransomware.

## 6. Recovery and Testing

Recovery plans should identify owners, dependencies, recovery sequence, credentials, vendors, restoration procedures, validation, and escalation. Test restoration and continuity procedures periodically according to risk.

## 7. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

Continuity planning may include alternate personnel, communication methods, manual processes, alternate technology, vendor alternatives, and remote-work capabilities. DR must address infrastructure loss, data corruption, cyber incidents, vendor outages, credential compromise, and extended disruption.

## 8. Third-Party and Client Requirements

Assess critical vendor continuity. Follow client backup, retention, recovery, testing, incident, and geographic requirements where applicable.

## 9. Incidents, Records, Exceptions, and Enforcement

Coordinate security-related recovery with the Security Incident Response Procedure and preserve evidence. Retain appropriate recovery records. Exceptions require authorization. Violations may result in corrective, contractual, or access-related action.
